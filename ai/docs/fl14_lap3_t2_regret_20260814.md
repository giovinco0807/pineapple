# FL14 lap3 T2 regret diagnosis — 2026-08-14

## Status

- Profile under study: `lap3_foul_margin_v1`
- `current` and the lap2 profile were not changed.
- The lap3 T2 teacher set contains 10,000 reached decisions, 241,206 legal
  actions, and all 17 reached board shapes.
- The original 104-dimensional diagnostic model is the ordinary SmoothL1
  regressor with seed `20260816`, selected on dev charged regret.
- The hand-list diagnosis produced a separate 110-dimensional feature candidate.
  A wider three-layer model improved its dev charged regret in all three seeds.
  Its paired 10,000-hand end-to-end gate is also complete.
- Because the old test hand list informed the new feature design, that split is
  now diagnostic-only for the 110-dimensional candidate and is not a holdout.
- The long-hand point estimate was negative, so neither the candidate nor its
  wider model was promoted.  `current` and lap2 remain unchanged.

## Corrected split identity

The lap2 concatenated labels reused a worker-local `root` ordinal 2,173 times.
That mixed unrelated decisions when computing splits and regret.  Lap3 groups
by the globally stable played-deal `id`; fit/dev/test contain 7,989 / 984 /
1,027 roots with no overlap.  Therefore lap2's old reported dev regret is not a
valid apples-to-apples training comparison, although lap2's end-to-end 10,000
hand baseline remains valid.

## Where regret concentrates

- A different discard from the teacher occurred in 243/1,027 roots and carried
  72.1% of total test regret.
- A different set of top-row cards occurred in 171/1,027 roots but carried
  50.8% of total regret.  Dev independently showed 57.4%.
- Shapes `1-3-3`, `1-2-4`, `2-2-3`, and `2-3-2` carried 78.7% of total test
  regret.
- Visible jokers were not the cause: mean regret was 0.337 / 0.356 / 0.338 for
  0 / 1 / 2 visible jokers.
- A simple missed-pair rule was not the cause.  Most regret remained in roots
  where the model discard did not pair a card already on the board.

The stable interpretation is a cross-row allocation problem, especially the
value of reserving, adding, or withholding cards from the top row.  It is not a
one-direction bias: the model sometimes underinvests in top and sometimes puts
a card there that should protect middle or bottom.

## Worst observed example

Root `3238002224`:

- Before action: top `Ad`; middle `Tc,Td,Th`; bottom `4s,7s,8s`; dead `7d`;
  draw `5d,6d,Ah`.
- Teacher: `Ad,Ah | 5d,Tc,Td,Th | 4s,7s,8s`, discard `6d`.
- Model: `Ad | Tc,Td,Th | 4s,5d,6d,7s,8s`, discard `Ah`.
- Original teacher values were +15.623 versus +7.956, charged regret 7.668.
- The model predicted +10.381 versus +12.342 and therefore discarded `Ah`.

The 104-dimensional input did distinguish these actions: 12 actor features,
21 rowwise-outlook features, and all 8 joint-outlook features changed.  This is
not a feature collision.  The model saw the top-pair/Fantasyland signal but
ranked the safer completed bottom too highly.

This root remains the largest diagnostic error under the 110-dimensional
candidate, with the same action and charged regret.  The added features address
the repeated rank-allocation collision pattern; they cannot repair every
high-regret decision by themselves.

## Teacher-noise check

The original worst 10 test roots were relabelled twice on independent streams
at 120 opponents, 96 T3 draws, and 24 T4 draws (twice the original count in
each dimension).

- Original selected-tail mean regret: 4.093.
- Mean regret using the average of the two high-sample relabels: 1.867.
- The two high-sample streams agreed on the best action in 6/10 roots.
- The original and both high-sample streams all agreed in 5/10 roots.
- The model was best under the averaged high-sample values in 1/10 roots; one
  further root was only 0.014 behind.

Thus the raw tail magnitude was substantially inflated by teacher sampling
noise, but the allocation weakness is real in roughly eight of the selected ten
roots.  These selected roots must not be used to estimate population regret.

## Feature correction from the hand list

Among dev roots where the 104-dimensional model chose the wrong action, 81.3%
had the same partial hand category but a different incomplete-row rank
signature; those roots carried 84.8% of dev regret.  The corresponding test
figures were 82.5% and 83.2%, but test is cited only as the source of the
hypothesis, not as independent confirmation.

The old 104 features are frozen as an exact prefix.  Six allocation-rank values
are appended in top/middle/bottom order, two per row:

- completed rows use the first two exact hand tie-break ranks;
- incomplete rows use category-aware multiplicity ranks, so pair rank precedes
  its kicker, two-pair ranks are high then low, and trips rank precedes kicker;
- a joker joins the largest/highest rank group, consistently in Python and
  Rust.

This makes examples such as exchanging a `2` and `3` between top and middle, or
choosing which kicker accompanies middle trips, directly visible to the model.
The existing 48 actor values were not redefined, so every old model keeps its
original meaning.  Two extra cross-row margin values were also tried on dev;
they worsened seed `20260816` regret to 0.312424 and were rejected.  The accepted
candidate therefore has 110 inputs, not 112.

## Compatibility and runtime proof

- The new encoded fit/dev/test matrices have 110 columns.  Columns 0--103,
  labels, root ids, and joker counts are bit-identical to the old corpus.
- The shipped Python and Rust encoders agreed exactly on all five blocks,
  including the new allocation-rank block: maximum absolute difference 0.
- Rebuilding the v2 runtime did not change a single old 104-dimensional parity
  value; the before/after report SHA-256 is
  `D079416CA99208D6A021B7482BA2B0ED9E74C6778814B6D9574630E0C2FD9BB8`.
- A mixed street smoke test played 1,000 deals with the unchanged 96-dimensional
  lap2 T0/T1 models and the new 110-dimensional T2 model, producing 1,000 unique
  T3 roots without a width or dispatch error.
- The v2 ranker variant is intentionally not introduced.  The existing
  96-dimensional ranker remains on the old schema.

## Diagnostic-only reread of the old test split

After the candidate was frozen on dev, the already-inspected test split was
rerun to find remaining failure modes.  Mean regret moved 0.343212 to 0.315119,
zero-regret rate 67.77% to 69.43%, and p90 1.306 to 1.209.  Of 1,027 roots, 63
improved, 45 worsened, 919 were unchanged, and the selected action changed in
108.  The p99 slightly worsened from 3.082 to 3.095 and the maximum stayed
7.668; root `3238003749` newly incurred 5.820 regret.  The correction therefore
improves the intended average signal but does not establish tail safety.

These figures are not an adoption metric because the hand list used to design
the feature came from this split.  The content-disjoint paired 10,000-hand gate
below is the adoption metric used for the frozen wider candidate.

## Second improvement pass

### Label-noise audit

The worst 15 dev roots were relabelled twice with independent streams, 120
opponents, 96 T3 draws, and 24 T4 draws.  The two streams agreed on the best
action in only 6/15 roots.  The apparent mean regret of the narrow 110 model
fell from 3.405 under the original labels to 0.812 under the two-stream average.
This confirms that the raw worst-hand tail is dominated by teacher noise even
though a smaller real residual remains.  On those same 15 diagnostic roots,
the frozen wider model reduced averaged high-sample regret from 0.812 to 0.621.
This selected subset is diagnostic evidence, not a population gate.

### Rejected feature extensions

The old 104 columns and the six-column v2 suffix were held fixed while each
candidate was screened on dev only.  Explicit texture, static ordering margins,
and a formally encoded placed-rank tail did not beat their 110-dimensional
comparator.  A 13-column completion-tiebreak block was then fully encoded as a
123-dimensional corpus, keeping columns 0--109, labels, roots, and joker counts
bit-identical.  It also lost with both network sizes:

| model, seed 20260816 | 110 dev regret | extended dev regret | result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| standard, completion tiebreak +13 | 0.309665 | 0.314388 | reject |
| wide, completion tiebreak +13 | 0.298324 | 0.308023 | reject |

The 123-dimensional runtime path was removed after this gate.  Only the
backward-compatible 104-dimensional v1 and 110-dimensional v2 encoders remain.

### Wider model gate

The winning change was model capacity rather than another feature.  Hidden
widths changed from `256,128,64` to `512,256,128`; data, fit-only
normalization, SmoothL1 loss, AdamW defaults, 80 epochs, and dev-regret
checkpoint selection stayed fixed.  Test was not read.

| seed | narrow 110 | wide 110 | delta |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20260814 | 0.310947 | 0.306884 | -0.004063 |
| 20260815 | 0.315668 | 0.310551 | -0.005117 |
| 20260816 | 0.309665 | 0.298324 | -0.011341 |
| mean | 0.312093 | 0.305253 | -0.006840 (-2.19%) |

The wider model won 3/3 seeds.  Reducing AdamW weight decay to 0.001 improved
only 2/3 seeds and was rejected despite a slightly better mean.  Averaging the
three wide predictions improved MAE but worsened charged regret to 0.314358,
so the ensemble was also rejected.  Seed `20260816`, with the default 0.01
weight decay, was frozen before the long-hand gate.

## Paired 10,000-hand lap2 gate

The measurement deals reproduce the established lap2 comparison set exactly.
Their first 11 cards had zero content overlap with the new 10,000-position T2
training corpus.  Both arms used the same lap2 T0/T1 models; only T2 changed.
All 10,000 ids and all 17 seen cards matched between arms, so the 240
Fantasyland opponents were identical deal by deal.  The T2 change reached a
different eleven-card board in 1,849 hands.

In this table, delta is wider 110 minus lap2 and the interval is the paired 95%
interval:

| metric | lap2 | wider 110 | paired delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| score vs FL opponent | -4.997 | -5.031 | -0.034 +/- 0.118 `[-0.152, +0.084]` |
| royalty per hand | 6.508 | 6.505 | -0.003 +/- 0.039 |
| foul rate | 26.72% | 26.82% | +0.10 pp +/- 0.316 pp |
| FL entry rate | 33.06% | 32.96% | -0.10 pp +/- 0.318 pp |
| T3 expected value | -5.195 | -5.176 | +0.019 +/- 0.049 |

The arm-specific worst-1% mean was -25.597 for lap2 and -25.592 for the wider
model; both minima were -27.217 and 94/100 bottom-tail hands overlapped.  Thus
there is no material tail regression in this run, but the primary realized
score point estimate is not positive and its interval does not establish a
gain.  Under the stated promotion rule the candidate fails.  No profile was
created or switched, and `current` remains untouched.  Any next candidate
chosen after inspecting this result needs a different fresh paired set for its
final promotion gate.
