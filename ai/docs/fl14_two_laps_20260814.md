# FL14 vs Fantasyland: two laps, and the first number that is not internal

2026-08-14 (JST). The joker track's normal-player-versus-Fantasyland chain, built
twice. This records what each lap was, what was wrong with the first one, and
what the second one is worth — measured by playing hands rather than by scoring
a model against its own teacher.

---

## The result

Ten thousand deals neither chain ever trained on, seventeen cards each, played
to completion by both. Paired: every deal is the same seventeen cards for both
arms, and — because a T4 hero has seen all seventeen however the chain arranged
them, and `pool::draw` is a function of that mask — **both arms were scored
against literally the same 240 Fantasyland opponents on every deal.** The paired
difference carries no opponent noise at all.

| per hand, n = 10,000 | lap one | lap two | paired difference (95%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **score vs the FL opponent** | −8.236 | −4.997 | **+3.239** [+2.858, +3.619] |
| royalty (a foul counts 0) | 5.931 | 6.508 | +0.577 [+0.442, +0.712] |
| foul rate | 31.35% | 26.72% | **−4.63 pt** [−5.61, −3.65] |
| Fantasyland entry rate | 27.93% | 33.06% | **+5.13 pt** [+4.16, +6.10] |
| Fantasyland EV credited | 5.573 | 7.934 | +2.361 [+2.115, +2.607] |

Fouls, head to head: lap one died on 1,503 deals lap two survived; lap two died
on 1,040 lap one survived; 1,632 killed both.

### Where the three points come from — and it is not royalty

Entry widths, and what each is worth under `ai/config/fl_ev.json`:

| width | top row | value | lap one | lap two |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 14 | QQ | **0** | 377 | 150 |
| 15 | KK | 10.7 | 907 | 898 |
| 16 | AA | 29.9 | 1,482 | **2,192** |
| 17 | trips | 63.5 | 27 | 66 |

Lap two enters Fantasyland 5.13 points more often, but that is the small half.
It stops entering on queens — worth literally nothing in this table, because the
number is a *surplus over playing a normal hand* and with jokers a normal hand
is strong — and enters on aces instead. **Lap two did not learn to build bigger
boards. It learned to build boards that qualify wider and die less.**

Decomposed: royalty +0.58, Fantasyland +2.36, lines and fouls +0.30.

A caveat that belongs next to the number: `fl_ev` is provisional
(`"source": "user_direct_20260613"`), and the same file's `fl_stats` implies
roughly 1.5x the table it ships. The closed-form fix for the circularity —
`V* = (V0 − s·x0)/(1 − s)`, and **do not iterate** — is established on the
regular track and has not been applied here. The *direction* (16 beats 14) does
not depend on the table; the split between the three terms does.

### Why the deals are clean

Seed `0xC0FFEE01`, 17 cards, filtered by content against twelve corpora — every
teacher's roots and labels for both laps, matched on the *cards seen* at 14, 11,
8 and 5, jokers collapsed, order ignored. Zero hits.

A fresh seed is **not** sufficient, and the counterexample turned up in the
first thousand: the T0 corpus was generated outside `deal`, so a fresh deal's
opening five can land on one by chance (root 176 did, about what C(54,5)
predicts). Deals are over-generated and dropped by root, so both arms stay on
identical deals.

## Lap one: what it was

Five streets, each labelled by the street below, against a **best-responding**
Fantasyland opponent — one that sets its thirteen cards after seeing hero's
finished board. (The rule correction that established this invalidated every
vs-FL asset built before 2026-08-11; the earlier ones scored against a
statically solved board, which flatters hero by 0.248 a hand on average and 5.0
at worst.)

| street | served by | regret | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T4 | solver, 0.9 ms | 0.0018 | opponents sampled, nothing else |
| T3 | solver, 0.26 s | 0.0017 | the T4 draw is fully enumerated |
| T2 | sampled solver, 4.78 s | 0.064 floor / 0.156 net | T3 draws, T4 draws and opponents all sampled |
| T1 | 96-dim net | 0.132 | bootstrap on the T2 net |
| T0 | 96-dim net | 0.068 | bootstrap on the T1 net |

**"Exact" is only honest at T3 and T4.** A fully enumerated T2 decision is
23.9 actions × C(43,3) T3 draws × 21 placements × C(40,3) T4 draws — 612 billion
terminals, 45 hours a hand at 60 opponents. The pair table that makes T3
affordable (780 pricings serve all 9,880 draws) has nothing to amortise over one
street up, because **the T3 draw is observed before the T3 choice**, so the
expectation does not factor.

### Where the ladder breaks, and what to do about it

Truncating a playout at a learned value pays exactly when the subtree costs more
than one feature encode:

| street | subtree per candidate | truncation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | tens of ms — three levels of sampling | **100x faster**, and floor 2.03 → 0.16 |
| T2 | 0.199 ms — one sampled completion, table-amortised | **1.3x slower** |

So T1 and T0 bootstrap and T2 does not. At T1 the "exact" alternative was
measured: 91 s a root at a sampling level whose floor was 2.03 — thirty times
the noise of the street below it — and about $390 of cloud time for 3,000 roots.

The consequence, stated plainly because it is easy to forget: **T1 and T0 labels
never consult the Fantasyland pool** (every one carries `mean_fl_samples: 0.0`).
Their regret measures fidelity to the street below, not correctness, and their
floors are near zero whether or not the answer is right.

### Two models per street

The 104-dim evaluator keeps the joint block and serves T2; the 96-dim one drops
it and is what a search can afford. The block is worth 0.085 of regret at T2 and
**98% of a candidate encode** — one T1 request measured 0.77 s without it and
48.6 s with. A street is served by the model that sees the most and searched by
the model that costs the least.

## What was wrong with lap one

### The action enumeration was missing 43% of every move

`open_patterns` returned only row pairs with `first <= second`, and its callers
put the first kept card in `pattern[0]`. Two cards going to two different rows
have two outcomes; one was never built. **A (2,4,3) board offers 21 T3 actions
and the labeler enumerated 12.**

Every FL14 value was a maximum over 57% of the legal moves — biased low, and a
teacher's "best action" was the best of a subset. Same twenty deals, only the
action set corrected: the best action gains **+0.696** and a root's spread goes
1.42 → 2.11.

Found because a key lookup missed on 37% of positions while measuring something
else. The number that lookup was about to produce would have been a mean over
the biased 63% that matched.

### The teachers labelled boards nobody plays

`teach` dealt its roots: `cards[0..2]` to the top, `[2..6]` to the middle,
`[6..9]` to the bottom. **All 30,000 T3 roots were shape (2,4,3).** Playing the
same fourteen cards reaches fourteen shapes, of which (2,4,3) is 32%.

The shapes were the visible half. A dealt root's actions sat **1.53** apart; a
played root's sit **9.49** apart. Thirty thousand roots went on decisions that
barely mattered — which is also why lap one's gates read well.

Same defect at T2 (`(2,3,2)`, 14.4% of what play reaches). T1 differs: its
`sample_t1_root` randomises the row counts, so all 18 shapes appear equally —
but the T0 model reaches **(1,2,2) 59.3%** of the time and the teacher gave that
shape 5.5% of its labels. T0 is immune: five cards, no board to arrange.

Measured, same test split, both models:

| | trained on played roots | trained on dealt roots | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T3 | 0.0905 (p99 2.26) | 0.5753 (p99 10.82) | **6.4x** |
| T2 | 0.338 | 1.709 (p99 11.77) | **5.1x** |

The loss lives in the tail: a dealt-trained model gives up ten points at the
99th percentile on shapes it never saw.

### Four smaller ones, each of which produced plausible output

- **The two jokers are the same card.** `Card { rank: 0, suit: 4 }` twice, so a
  harvest comparing by value let a draw holding one joker acquire the two-joker
  placement. 2.58% of harvested T4 actions were illegal, and in **7 of the 10
  roots touched, the illegal action was the best one**.
- **A noise floor of exactly 0.0.** `draw` derives its cursor and stride modulo
  the pool size, so streams differing by a multiple of it walk the same entries.
  The floor pass ran at `--stream-offset 1000000` against a 200,000-entry pool
  and reproduced the first pass bit for bit. A floor of zero for a sampled
  teacher is not a plausible reading, which is what made it worth checking.
- **The harvest and the opponents shared a stream**, so a second labelling pass
  harvested different decisions and the two passes shared almost no keys — the
  one thing a second pass exists to provide.
- **60 opponents leaves 1.82 at the leaf.** The draw conditions on the root's
  seen cards; the leaf re-filters against everything the playout revealed.
  Survival is ~2.9% at T1. Labels still appear, no error is raised, and only
  comparing values reveals it. (It turned out not to matter for *ranking* —
  common random numbers make the opponent draw common-mode — but that was luck,
  not design.)

## Lap two: what changed

Same five streets, with the enumeration corrected, roots taken from positions
the lap-one chain actually reaches, and the joker constraint no longer setting
the wall time.

| street | teacher | roots | gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2 | 5,000 played positions, 17 shapes | `lap2_t2_teacher` | 0.338 |
| T1 | 3,000 played positions | `lap2_t1_labels` | 0.298 |
| T0 | 1,000 canonical hands | `lap2_t0_labels` | 0.207 |

**These are not comparable to lap one's own gates.** Lap one measured on dealt
boards where twelve actions sat 1.53 apart; these are played boards where they
sit 9.49 apart. A model that ranks equally well scores worse on the harder set.
Only the same-split comparisons above, and the head-to-head at the top, say
anything.

T3 was dropped from the lap: its model has no consumer. The solver serves the
street at 0.26 s against a floor of 0.0017 where the net was 0.09; the playout
never reaches the T3 chooser because T1 and T0 truncate at depth 0; and
truncating T2 there is slower. Its *labels* still matter — they are what a T3
gate reads — but a few hundred roots buy that.

### T0 samples canonical hands

C(54,5) = 3,162,510 deals collapse to **152,646 classes** under suit relabelling
and the joker swap (cross-checked by Burnside, not against itself). One label
therefore speaks for 20.7 deals, and 1,000 of them cover 20,992. Multiplicities
are bounded and bimodal — 2 to 48, six distinct values, 93% at 12 or 24 — so
deal-weighting moves the sampled population by 16%, not by orders of magnitude.
T0 is the only street whose state space is small enough for this to mean
anything.

## Performance: 94% of the time was in a place nobody had looked

Splitting a T3 solve with `--own-only`, which skips opponent scoring entirely:
1042 ms a root scored, 980 ms unscored. **The best-response frontier scan — the
thing that looked expensive — is 6-8%.** Terminal evaluation is the rest.

Three changes, all verified **byte-identical** on labels across six
configurations (0/1/2 jokers, all fourteen played board shapes, `--own-only`,
240 opponents, `--stream-offset`):

1. **A row memo in `fl_solver`.** `completion_value` cloned the base board and
   re-evaluated all three rows for each of ~33,000 completions a root, though a
   placement moves two cards. Rebuilt rather than lifted from
   `t4_first_exact`'s: the dependency arrow already points the other way.
   *Thread-seconds a root: played 1.63 → 0.23.*
   Two things the port could not copy — reading `has_joker` off the cached row
   evaluation made it **70% slower than no memo at all**, and the second cache
   level cannot hit because each (pair, placement) is visited once.
2. **The constrain searches, in `ofc_core`.** `constrain_5_vs_5` and
   `constrain_3_vs_5` read their reference row **only through its value**
   (`compare_5_hands` is `sign(a − b)`; `available_subs` ignores the reference),
   so each is a pure function of `(cards, reference value)`. The property is now
   a test, checked against a verbatim copy of the pre-change search — asking the
   cached function the same question twice returns the first answer whatever the
   premise is worth.
3. **`JOKER_EVAL_CACHE` was read and never written**, at HEAD and since it was
   authored; its only symptom was a dead `_LIMIT` constant among 26 warnings. A
   two-joker row with no legal substitution comes back still holding its jokers,
   so three separate call sites re-solve all 990 pairs for it, every completion.
   Restoring the insert alone: 26.3 s → 3.6 s.

| | before | after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| T2 label, a root | 4.78 s | **1.25 s** |
| root with two jokers in the middle | 97.8 s | **2.0 s** |
| root with one joker in the middle | 8.7 s | 0.86 s |
| `ofc_core` golden joker suite | 9.83 s | 3.21 s |

A two-joker root went from 150x a joker-free one to 4x, so one of them no longer
decides how long a batch takes.

`teach` also publishes a chunk at a time now (250 roots, parallel within,
sequential between) — it collected everything and wrote once, a shape that has
cost this project an hour of preempted work twice. Chunking rather than writing
from inside the parallel map, because thread-ordered lines would retire the
byte-equality check every optimisation above was accepted on.

## What is still open

**Correctness / method**

- Labels carry no standard error, so a noise floor costs a second labelling
  pass. The regular track's carry one and compute the floor by resampling each
  candidate from `N(ev, se)` twice. Measuring the T1 floor here cost 35 minutes.
- The T1 teacher's roots are shape-uniform, not on-policy. Lap two fixed the
  positions but not the sampler.
- Whether truncating at T1 beats playing out is **still unsettled**. On four
  positions the two disagreed on every one — the truncated shape building the
  top row where the exact one spread — but the exact reference at that budget
  does not reproduce its own argmax between seeds.

**Speed**

- `--play-roots` exhausts 64 GB after 32 chunks and runs fine on any one of
  them, so something accumulates across them; the smaller chunk size only moves
  the wall.
- The card representation is `Card { rank, suit }` in `Vec`s converted between
  two identical types per call — roughly 230,000 heap allocations a root. Rank
  nibbles and per-suit masks in two u64s would make this allocation-free and
  incremental. Worth measuring only after the above, since the memos removed
  most of what it would speed up.
- `fl_solver`'s `lib.rs` and `main.rs` are duplicate copies.

**Not started**

- Pools for widths 15, 16 and 17 (arrangement counts 7.5x, 40x, 170x of 14's).
- `fl_ev` is provisional and the circularity fix has not been applied here.
- Three things from the regular track, which gets a T0 label in **3.77 s**
  without truncating anything: sharing the draw tree across actions (the
  per-root fixed cost does not depend on the opening), `--narrow-keep` (232
  openings to 20 is 13.9x, but only once the fixed cost is gone — measured 1.42x
  before), and the standard error above.

**The largest one**

Fouls. Both chains foul about a third of the time, and every one of them was
**forced** — `unfouled == 0` on all 307 lap-one and 272 lap-two fouls in the
thousand-deal run, so the T4 solver never chose a foul over a legal finish. But
only ~28% were already lost at T3. **The other ~72% held a live board that the
particular three-card T4 draw killed.** A 30% foul rate is a property of how
T0-T3 leave room, not of the last street, and nothing in either lap targets it
directly.
