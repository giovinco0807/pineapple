## Why this lap is different

Lap two already fixed the legal-action set and trained on played positions.  A
third copy of the same ladder would mostly buy another sample from the same
method.  The largest measured loss is now the final foul: lap two fouled 26.72%
of 10,000 unseen deals.  Every realised foul was forced on the particular T4
draw (`unfouled == 0`), and most of those positions were still live before that
draw.  The target is therefore the room T0--T3 leave for the last three cards,
not a different T4 tie-break.

The scalar objective does not change.  A foul is already priced in the actual
score against the best-responding Fantasyland opponent.  Foul rate is a
diagnostic and guardrail, not a replacement reward that can trade away more EV
than it saves.

  --finished D:/ofc_data/lap_compare_10k/lap2_finished.jsonl `

It records whole-hand score, royalty, foul, entry rate and entry EV, plus the
T3 board shapes that contribute the realised forced fouls.  Its `unfouled`
counts describe the one T4 draw that occurred; they must not be reported as a
T3 position's full foul probability.

## Work order

1. **Measure before fitting.**  Produce the baseline receipt and identify the
   T3 shapes that contribute most of lap two's realised forced fouls.
2. **Make the teacher expose risk and uncertainty.**  Add a foul/survival
   decomposition first.  Then measure finite-opponent uncertainty without
   confusing the fully enumerated T4 draw distribution with label noise;
   common random numbers remain shared across actions.
3. **Refresh on policy.**  Play lap two to collect T1 and T2 roots.  Preserve
   the reached shape frequencies; do not replace them with a shape-uniform
   sampler.  T0 remains canonical and deal-weighted.
4. **Settle T1 truncation on a pilot.**  On matched on-policy roots, compare
   the current depth-zero bootstrap with a full playout using independent
   evaluation streams.  Advance the full path only if it improves held-out EV,
   not merely foul rate.  Shared draw trees and narrowing are performance
   work in service of this experiment, not assumptions about its answer.
5. **Rebuild bottom up.**  Train T2, then T1, then T0.  Keep explicit
   `lap3_foul_margin_v1_t{0,1,2}` artifact names and never overwrite lap two.
6. **Freeze, then open the final gate.**  Generate 10,000 content-disjoint
   seventeen-card deals from `0xC0FFEE31`; play both arms on every deal and
   score both against literally the same 240 opponents.

## Promotion gate

Primary:

* paired score difference, lap three minus lap two: 95% interval entirely
  above zero.

Guardrails:

* paired foul-rate difference: no statistically clear regression;
* paired Fantasyland-entry EV: no statistically clear regression;
* no chosen T4 foul (`busted && unfouled > 0`);
* the worst 1% paired-score tail does not regress by more than 0.25 points per
  hand;
* all training and final-gate prefixes pass content-based disjointness.

Royalty and entry width remain decomposition metrics.  They are not separate
promotion votes: the whole-hand paired score decides.

## First stop/go decision

The first implementation gate is the uncertainty/risk label contract, followed
by the matched T1 truncation pilot.  Do not launch a full lap-three corpus or
cloud fleet before those two readings exist.  If the full T1 path does not win
independently, lap three continues with on-policy refresh and better measured
bootstrap labels instead of forcing the expensive method into production.
