# T0 first-seat openings — handoff, 2026-08-21

Where this stands, what is settled, what was retracted, and what is still
running. Read the retractions section before trusting any number you find
elsewhere: several figures quoted confidently earlier in this work turned out to
be wrong, and the wrong versions are still readable in commit messages.

## The one finding everything else rests on

**A single 1,024-particle batch cannot rank T0 actions.** The top-two gap
scatters with SD 0.475 at 1,024 particles and 0.231 at 4,096 — measured over
twelve openings and confirmed against the square-root law to within 3%. So one
batch separates two actions only when their true gap exceeds about 0.9.

Of the twenty-four openings solved with one batch each on 2026-08-19, nine clear
that bar. **The other fifteen were never ranked**, and the "mean regret 0.107
points a hand" derived from them is roughly four times its true value — the
maximum of thirty noisy estimates is biased upward by construction.

Canonical: `docs/t0_particle_noise_20260820.md`.

## What is measured and standing

**19 of 24 openings settled** in the lookup table, deepest carrying 132,096
particles. `python -m ofc_regular.hu_t0_solved_openings_v1 --list`.

**The shipped T0 first-seat policy picks the measured best on 15 of 17**
converged openings, mean cost 0.029 points a hand. The two misses are the two
that took the most particles to decide (81k and 45k, costing 0.218 and 0.283);
everything it got right converged in 3k to 35k. **It is reliable where the
answer is obvious and unreliable where it is not.** The sample is biased toward
easy hands and the direction is measured, not the magnitude.

**The model is a better narrowing stage than the sieve.** Across the seventeen
settled openings it placed the measured best no deeper than third of 232. The
sieve's top-ten once discarded eight candidates that were still within four
sigma after a batch.

**The model's EV output is not usable as a value.** Correlation 0.841 over 768
candidates, MAE 4.11 points, and it reads about 5 points high in the band that
matters. On one settled hand it scored the three contenders 4.88 / 4.40 / 3.31
against measured 0.530 / 0.083 / 0.715 — the order reversed.

**T0 first seat has 134,459 canonical openings** (Burnside over the 24 suit
permutations; 2,598,960 raw). Suit symmetry holds only at this street: once the
first seat has placed, it is gone, and T0 second seat is 1,533,939 deals per
first-seat position.

## Retractions — do not re-derive these

Each of these was stated with confidence and is wrong. They are listed because
the wrong version is quoted in earlier commits and docs.

| claimed | actual |
|---|---|
| "the NN forward pass is ~138% of playout cost, GPU could help" | profile says 9.1% forward pass, 72.7% outlook. The GPU port fails the brief's own 20% rule |
| "the gap was overstated 1.89x" | 4.0x. The 1.89 came from four batches that happened to agree |
| "seed spacing correlates the batches" | ANOVA F(2,9)=5.61 but the arm means do not order by spacing. Not supported |
| "`As Kd Kh Ts 7s` needs 2,529k particles" | then 63k, then 107k. Particles-needed goes as the inverse square of the gap, so a projection at 2 sigma moves by a factor of forty. **Do not quote particles_needed below 3 sigma** |
| "the model does not fail more on hard hands" | it does, above about 45k particles. The sample was too small and too easy when that was written |
| "the sieve was paying for itself" | the probe's control arm scored 232 candidates instead of 32. Invalid comparison |
| "there is a 1,812 core-s fixed cost per call" | doubling particles doubles cost exactly (x1.99 measured). No intercept. The candidate-count effect is real but it is cache amortisation across candidates in one call, not a fixed cost |

The pattern in all of them: a two-point extrapolation, or a statistic computed
before enough samples existed. **Three sigma before quoting, and never fit a
line through two points that differ in more than one way.**

## What is paused, and how to resume

**apair50** — 50 openings of the form A + one pair + two further ranks, sampled
from 13,200 canonical (seed 20260820). 7 batches on disk, 0 converged.

```
python -m ofc_regular.hu_t0_elimination_runner_v1 \
  --hands D:/ofc_data/t0_rung/apair50/hands.json \
  --out   D:/ofc_data/t0_rung/apair50/batches \
  --worker N --workers 3 --keep 32 --budget 40
```

`--keep 32`, not 15. Per-evaluation cost falls sharply with more candidates in
one call (0.150 core-s at 15, 0.037 at 232), so a wider keep buys roughly twice
the candidates for about 13% more time — and closes the question of whether a
true best sits outside the cut. Do **not** raise `--samples` instead; that was
measured and it is exactly linear.

`RAYON_NUM_THREADS=5` with three workers filled 13.5 of 16 cores. Prefer thread
counts that divide the candidate count.

**akq20 ladder** — 17 of 20 converged, three left open by choice at gaps of 0.11
to 0.18, which is less than the model's own error on the hands it gets wrong.

## Import order, which will bite you

The patched `ofc_regular` lives in the worktree. Adding the repository's `src`
to `sys.path` puts an unpatched copy ahead of it and silently removes
`restrict_action_keys` from `JointExactConfig`. Add the repository **root** for
`trainer`, the worktree's `src` for `ofc_regular`, and copy any repo-only module
into the worktree rather than adding the second `src`.

## Open questions, in the order worth answering

1. **How many openings does the model actually need?** Nobody knows and it
   decides everything downstream. The model is 32,349 float32 parameters and the
   table's raw information content is 129 KB, so memorisation is arithmetically
   impossible and it must generalise or grow. The project's own T1 evidence had
   the learning curve flat by 18,000 roots. About $700 of labels buys a curve at
   750/1,500/3,000 and tells you whether the target is 10,000 or 100,000.
2. **Is the full table worth building at all?** At the elimination method's cost
   it projects to roughly $31,000 of GCP Spot — and that rests on a per-particle
   cost known only as a lower bound. If the answer to (1) is 10,000 openings,
   the table is moot.
3. **Why does per-evaluation cost fall fourfold from 15 candidates to 232?**
   Arm 2 of the running probe addresses the rayon half. If it is cache
   amortisation instead, batching more candidates per call is the lever and the
   elimination method should keep a wider field longer than it does.
